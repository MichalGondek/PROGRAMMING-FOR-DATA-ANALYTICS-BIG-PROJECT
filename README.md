# PROGRAMMING-FOR-DATA-ANALYTICS-BIG-PROJECT
# Analysis of Rainfall and Home Insurance Trends in Ireland (2024-2025)
# Author: Michal Gondek

## I work in Aviva Insurance Ireland and wanted to make my main project relevant to my work

## Project Overview
- This project analyses monthly rainfall data across five Irish weather stations and compares it to the home insurance premium index.
- The aim is to demonstrate how extreme weather events and increased rainfall influence home insurance premiums 

## Data Sources used for comparison
- Met Eireann monthly weather data: JSON data for five stations
- CSO Consumer Price Index
- Storm Eowyn news article

## Project Structure
- Importing and Cleaning Data
- Compare Annual Rainfall
- Identify Extreme Weather Months
- Visualise Rainfall Data by Station
- Monthly Rainfall Trend Analysis
- National Summary Using Mean Monthly Rainfall
- Extreme Rainfall vs Insurance Premiums
- Storm Annotation to Highlight Storm Eowyn Impact

## Key Results and Insights
- Four out of five stations had higher rainfall in 2025 compared to 2024
- Extreme rainfall months increased from 3 in 2024 to 7 in 2025
- The insurance premium index rose by 7.5% between 2024 and 2025
- Storm Eowyn impact costing insurance sector over 300 million euro.

## Conclusion
This project examined monthly rainfall patterns across five weather stations in Ireland and its potential impact on home insurance premiums.
Extreme weather conditions and storms have been a leading cause of increased premiums coming from the insurance sector where I currently work.
During storm Eowyn my company Aviva reported higher volume of insurance claim payouts within a single week than typically observed over a three month period.
My analysis demonstrated a measurable increase in both average rainfall and frequency of extreme rainfall months between 2024-2025.
The results show that four out of five stations recorded higher mean monthly rainfall indicating a nationwide trend rather than isolated local events.
This rise in extreme weather aligns with the observed 7.5% increase in home insurance premiums.
Overall I believe this analysis supports the conclusion that increased rainfall and storm activity factors to higher insurance costs in Ireland.
